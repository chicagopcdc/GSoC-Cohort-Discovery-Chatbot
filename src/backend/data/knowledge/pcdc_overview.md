## What PCDC is

PCDC stands for the Pediatric Cancer Data Commons, run by Data for the Common Good (D4CG) at the University of Chicago. PCDC pools clinical research data on childhood, adolescent and young adult, and adult cancers that would otherwise stay locked in separate trial datasets. Much of what PCDC holds comes from completed clinical trials contributed through disease-specific consortia. The point of pooling it is that a single cohort query can reach across studies and across diseases, which is what PCDC exists to make possible.

## The PCDC Data Portal

The portal at portal.pedscommons.org is where the harmonized data gets explored. Researchers use it to see which variables exist, build a cohort out of filters, and check whether enough subjects match to make a study worth pursuing. A free account is needed to explore. The counts it returns are for feasibility work and early hypotheses, not for publication.

## Data dictionaries and harmonization

Every disease area has its own data dictionary, agreed by clinicians in that consortium together with the National Cancer Institute. The dictionary fixes what each clinical data element means and which values it may take. That is what makes trials run decades apart comparable at all: when a study is loaded into PCDC its fields are mapped onto the dictionary rather than kept in the original study's own vocabulary.

## How many subjects the current release holds

The release currently indexed, pcdc_20260714_1, holds 46,647 subjects in total. That is how large the dataset is today: neuroblastoma through INRG accounts for many more subjects than any other consortium, 26,529 of them, with INSTRuCT next at 10,311 subjects. INTERACT, NODAL and MaGIC make up the remaining subjects. How big the dataset is shifts with each new release, so treat these totals as a snapshot rather than a fixed figure.
